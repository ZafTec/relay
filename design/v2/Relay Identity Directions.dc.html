Three directions for Relay by ZafTech

Each direction expresses movement through durable stages differently — in concept, composition and typographic character, not only in color. Marks below are real vector geometry drawn on a 32-unit grid, shown at review scale and at 32 / 24 / 16 px. Recommendation is at the bottom: read it here.

1A Direction A · Signal infrastructure

Handoff

Rails and a stepped path

Three fixed rails stand for durable stages — object, version, job. A single stepped path enters from outside the frame, climbs one step at each rail, and exits past the right edge: the work continues after the request that started it ends. The rails never move; the path is the only thing that changes. Composition is asymmetric and horizontal, so the mark reads as transit rather than as a container.

1B Direction B · Immutable layers · SELECTED

Ledger

Offset slabs, newest on top

Four slabs of equal weight stack with a fixed rightward offset. Nothing is overwritten: earlier versions stay visible beneath, and only the newest slab carries the accent. The mark is compact and vertical, which makes it strong in an app rail and in a browser tab, and it maps literally onto the product's asset-version model.

1C Direction C · Industrial editorial

Plate

Notched plate, baton through

A solid plate with a notch cut out of its right edge, and a bar passing through the notch and beyond. The identity's weight sits in the typography and technical notation — an uppercase mono wordmark with part-number annotation — while the mark stays quiet and stamped, the way a plant identifies equipment. Most "infrastructure company," least "developer dashboard."

// 02 Selected direction

Direction B — Ledger Owner choice

Ledger is the strongest single object of the three and maps literally onto the product's asset-version model: four slabs, fixed rightward offset, accent on the newest only. It is compact and vertical, so it holds in a browser tab and an app rail, and it reuses the exact slab geometry already drawn inside the version and hero diagrams — the identity and the asset family are one system rather than two.

Three rules keep it clear of database, layer, and blockchain marks — they are binding, not suggestions:

  • Offset stays aggressive (2.5 units per slab). A tight stack reads as a database.
  • Accent marks the newest slab only — never a gradient across the stack, never all four.
  • Slab count reduces with size: four at ≥ 24px, two at ≤ 20px, including the favicon.

Two elements carry forward from the other directions: Plate's mono notation for metadata (version, commit, job id), and a light paper surface for public pages so / can never be confused with /dashboard. The scoring below is the design pass's own read — recorded as-is; the owner selected B.
